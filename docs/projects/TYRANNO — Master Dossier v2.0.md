## 01. Project Overview

### Project Definition

TYRANNO is a speculative communication system that explores how humans and Tyrannosaurus rex might establish a shared frame of reference within a future context of coexistence.

The project begins from a condition of radical difference. Humans and T. rex do not share a language, bodily scale, behavioural repertoire, or inherited communication protocol. Under these conditions, translation cannot be treated as the simple conversion of one existing language into another. Before translation can occur, the conditions that make mutual interpretation possible must first be designed.

TYRANNO responds by developing a pictographic symbol system grounded in the observable body, posture, movement, and behaviour of T. rex. The animal is not only the recipient of communication. It becomes the principal reference through which meaning is constructed.

### One-Sentence Definition

> TYRANNO is a speculative pictographic communication system that uses T. rex behaviour as a shared reference for negotiating meaning and relationship across species.

### Project Positioning

TYRANNO operates across several connected fields:

- speculative design;
- communication design;
- semiotics;
- visual language systems;
- narrative world-building;
- interactive learning;
- digital storytelling.

The project uses an impossible or radically unfamiliar encounter to examine a familiar communication problem: meaning does not remain stable simply because a message has been sent.

### Authorial Role

Kylie's role is best described as:

- Speculative Communication Designer;
- Visual System Developer;
- Narrative World-builder.

Her contribution connects research, conceptual framing, symbolic development, visual system design, interaction concepts, and the construction of a coherent speculative context.

## 02. Project Abstract

Communication is often described as the transmission of information from a sender to a receiver. This description is incomplete. A message may be carefully expressed and successfully delivered while still being understood in a way the sender did not intend. The existence of a signal does not guarantee the existence of shared meaning.

TYRANNO examines this instability through an extreme cross-species context. In a speculative future where humans and T. rex occupy the same environment, neither participant can assume that the other shares its language, scale, physical expression, or way of interpreting the world. The problem is therefore not how to translate human language for a dinosaur. The problem is how to create enough common reference for communication to begin.

The project develops a pictographic system in which meaning is anchored to T. rex itself. Its body, posture, action, and behaviour become observable points of reference. Symbols are not invented as arbitrary replacements for human words. They are derived from relationships between the animal, the action being represented, the meaning assigned to that action, and the person attempting to communicate.

Through this approach, TYRANNO reframes communication as a relational structure rather than a one-directional act of transmission. The system does not claim to remove ambiguity or produce perfect mutual understanding. It creates a designed condition in which two fundamentally different species may begin to interpret, learn, respond, and coexist.

## 03. The Original Question

The project began with a speculative question:

> How might humans communicate with T. rex within a future condition of coexistence?

At first, this question appeared to invite a typographic answer: design a typeface, alphabet, or visual language that humans could use to communicate with the animal.

The project developed by challenging the assumptions contained in that first formulation.

A typeface presumes a writing system. A writing system presumes a language. Translation presumes that stable meanings already exist on both sides and can be converted between them. None of those conditions can be assumed in a cross-species encounter.

The question therefore evolved:

> What must exist before two fundamentally different species can interpret one another at all?

This shift moved the project away from designing a visual form for an existing message and towards designing the conditions under which meaning might become shareable.

## 04. Why This Project Must Exist

TYRANNO exists because communication cannot be guaranteed by expression alone.

A sender may believe that a message is precise. A receiver may believe that an interpretation is reasonable. Both may be acting coherently within their own systems of reference, yet the meaning that passes between them may continue to drift.

This problem is intensified in TYRANNO's speculative context:

- the participants belong to different species;
- their bodies operate at radically different scales;
- their behaviours cannot be read through the same assumptions;
- they do not share vocabulary or syntax;
- they do not inherit a common cultural code;
- neither side can rely on an established standard for checking meaning.

Under these conditions, human language is not a neutral solution. It would position the human system as the default and require the other species to enter it. Ordinary translation would also be insufficient because there is no verified equivalent language on the other side.

TYRANNO therefore addresses a prior problem: the absence of a shared frame of reference.

A shared frame of reference does not mean that both participants become the same or understand everything identically. It means that meaning is connected to something each participant can encounter and recognise. In TYRANNO, that common reference is not an abstract human vocabulary. It is the observable presence of T. rex: its form, movement, posture, and behaviour.

The project must exist because it creates a possibility that did not previously exist. It does not promise complete communication. It establishes a first condition for communication to be attempted.

## 05. Communication Philosophy

### Communication Is Not Delivery

The successful delivery of a message is not the same as successful communication.

Communication becomes effective only when the sender's intended meaning and the receiver's interpretation are sufficiently aligned for a response, decision, or relationship to proceed. Perfect equivalence may be impossible, but some shared standard is required if interpretation is not to remain entirely arbitrary.

### Shared Reference Makes Interpretation Negotiable

TYRANNO proposes that communication depends less on shared language than on shared reference.

Language is one highly developed way of organising shared reference, but it is not the only possible starting point. Where language does not yet exist, meaning may begin through visible relationships between bodies, actions, conditions, and consequences.

The project therefore follows this conceptual sequence:

```text
observable behaviour
        ↓
shared reference
        ↓
negotiated interpretation
        ↓
communication
        ↓
relationship
```

This sequence is not presented as a universal scientific model. It is the design logic through which TYRANNO constructs its speculative system.

### Communication Is Relational

TYRANNO treats relationship as both the purpose and the substance of communication.

The system creates several relationships simultaneously:

- between a symbol and the T. rex behaviour from which it is derived;
- between a symbol and the meaning it is intended to hold;
- between the symbol and the human who learns to use it;
- between the human and T. rex within a shared environment;
- between communication and the possibility of coexistence.

The symbols are therefore not isolated signs. Their meaning comes from the network of relationships they organise.

### Difference Is Not a Defect to Be Removed

The project does not attempt to make T. rex behave like a human speaker, nor does it imagine that human language should become the universal standard. Difference is the condition that gives the project its purpose.

TYRANNO asks how communication might be designed without erasing the other participant's physical and behavioural context. The system begins with the other species rather than treating it as an incomplete version of the human.

### Context Comes Before Form

The context determines the communication problem.

Once the species, environment, bodily scale, and conditions of coexistence change, familiar design assumptions must be re-examined. Typography, interface, interaction, and narrative cannot be selected independently of that context. They must grow from it.

In TYRANNO, the visual system is not the beginning of the project. It is the material consequence of a contextual and relational investigation.

## 06. The Design Challenge

The central challenge was not to invent an attractive speculative alphabet. It was to construct a visual system that could make unfamiliar meaning more stable without pretending that a complete cross-species language had been solved.

This required the project to address four linked questions:

1. What can act as a reference when the participants share no language?
2. How can a symbol remain connected to the species it concerns?
3. How much complexity can a participant reasonably be asked to learn before communication becomes inaccessible?
4. How can the project represent a possibility without presenting speculation as scientific certainty?

The design challenge can be summarised as:

> Before translation is possible, communication itself must exist.

TYRANNO therefore designs an entry point rather than a total solution.

## 07. Why Pictographic Symbols?

### From Typeface to Symbol System

The term *typeface* was useful during the project's early development but became too narrow and technically misleading.

A typeface is normally a designed set of letterforms within an existing writing system. TYRANNO does not simply restyle an alphabet. It constructs pictographic symbols whose visual logic is connected to bodies, actions, and behaviours.

The more accurate description is:

> a pictographic communication system.

### Why Not an Alphabet?

An alphabet would import a human linguistic structure into the project before the cross-species communication problem had been addressed. It would risk turning T. rex into the subject of a human code rather than the basis of a shared reference.

The project instead begins with observable phenomena. A form can point towards an action, posture, or behavioural condition before it becomes part of a larger linguistic system.

### Why Pictographic Logic?

Pictographic logic allows visual form to retain a visible relationship with what it refers to.

This does not make the meaning universal or self-evident. Every symbol still requires interpretation and learning. However, the connection between referent and representation is less arbitrary than a wholly invented letterform detached from the animal's body or behaviour.

In TYRANNO:

```text
T. rex body or behaviour
        ↓
observed relationship
        ↓
visual abstraction
        ↓
pictographic symbol
        ↓
learned communicative use
```

### Historical Reference and Semiotic Research

The project draws on research into pictographic and early symbolic systems, including oracle-bone forms, as references for how observation, image, abstraction, and meaning can become structurally connected.

These references are not used to imitate a historical script or claim equivalence between distinct cultures and a speculative cross-species system. Their value lies in demonstrating that visual signs can retain traces of bodies, actions, objects, and relationships while becoming repeatable components of communication.

Semiotic research supports the project's attention to the relationship between:

- sign;
- referent;
- interpretation;
- context;
- user.

### T. rex as the Reference Scale

The most important decision is that the symbol system takes T. rex itself as its reference scale.

The animal is not represented through generic human icons. Its physical structure and observable behaviour guide the formation of meaning. This reduces the degree to which the system depends on an exclusively human linguistic frame.

The pictographic system is therefore not simply about depicting a dinosaur. It uses the species as the ground against which symbols can be interpreted.

## 08. Core Design Principles

### 01. Begin With Context

Every design decision must respond to the speculative condition in which communication occurs. The system cannot be separated from species, environment, scale, behaviour, and coexistence.

### 02. Use Shared Reference Before Shared Language

The project does not assume that language already exists. It first establishes observable references around which meaning can be negotiated.

### 03. Anchor Meaning in the Other Species

T. rex is not a decorative subject. Its body and behaviour provide the source material and reference scale for the communication system.

### 04. Derive Form From Behaviour

Visual symbols should emerge from observable actions, postures, or physical relationships rather than arbitrary styling.

### 05. Reduce, but Do Not Deny, Ambiguity

No visual system can guarantee identical interpretation. TYRANNO aims to reduce uncontrolled meaning drift by making the basis of each sign more explicit.

### 06. Preserve Difference

Communication should not require the non-human participant to become human-like. The system creates a point of contact while respecting fundamental difference.

### 07. Keep Entry Possible

The system must be learnable enough for communication to begin. Complexity that prevents participation contradicts the project's purpose.

### 08. Treat Interaction as Part of Meaning

The system is not complete when symbols are displayed. Meaning develops through learning, testing, response, and repeated use.

### 09. Use Speculation Responsibly

TYRANNO creates a designed proposition, not a scientific claim that human–T. rex communication has been solved.

### 10. Let Visual Consistency Support Conceptual Consistency

Symbols, typography, motion, interface, narrative, and world-building should operate as parts of one communication ecology rather than independent visual outputs.

## 09. Speculative Context

TYRANNO is situated in a future-oriented environment in which humans and T. rex are imagined as occupying a shared urban condition.

This setting is not merely a fictional backdrop. It changes the practical and ethical questions the communication system must address.

If the two species share space, communication becomes connected to:

- proximity;
- safety;
- interpretation of behaviour;
- negotiation of boundaries;
- learning;
- choice;
- coexistence.

The speculative context allows the project to externalise communication problems that are often invisible in familiar human settings. In everyday communication, shared conventions are easily taken for granted. TYRANNO removes those conventions and asks what must be rebuilt.

The dinosaur functions as more than a dramatic subject. Its radical difference makes assumptions visible. It prevents the project from relying too quickly on existing language, familiar interface conventions, or human-centred models of interaction.

## 10. Research Foundation

The research combines several domains in order to construct a coherent speculative proposition.

### Communication Theory

Communication research established the difference between sending information and producing sufficiently aligned interpretation. This distinction led to the project's focus on shared reference.

### Semiotics

Semiotics provided a framework for considering how signs relate to referents, meanings, interpreters, and contexts. It also clarified that a sign is never meaningful in isolation.

### Pictographic and Symbolic Systems

Research into pictographic forms and oracle-bone references informed the investigation of how bodies, actions, and objects may be abstracted into repeatable visual signs.

### T. rex Body and Behaviour

Paleontological and behavioural references supported the construction of a system grounded in the animal rather than in cinematic cliché alone. The body, posture, movement, scale, and researched understanding of vocalisation all affected design decisions.

### Speculative Design and World-building

Speculative design provided a way to develop a coherent future condition without presenting it as prediction. World-building connected the symbol system to an environment in which it could be learned and used.

### Interaction and Learning

Interaction research shifted the website from a display surface into part of the communication system. Learning, recognition, and use became necessary components of the experience.

### Research Synthesis

The research was not treated as a collection of separate visual references. Its purpose was to connect:

```text
species context
        +
communication problem
        +
semiotic structure
        +
visual abstraction
        +
interaction
        =
speculative communication system
```

## 11. Methodology

TYRANNO developed through a movement from context towards system.

### 1. Define the Context

The project first establishes the participants, their differences, and the future condition in which communication becomes necessary.

### 2. Identify the Communication Gap

The absence of shared language reveals a deeper absence: there is no shared standard against which meaning can be checked.

### 3. Locate a Common Reference

T. rex itself becomes the most credible shared reference available within the project. Its observable body and behaviour can be encountered by both the system's human users and the speculative non-human participant.

### 4. Translate Behaviour Into Visual Structure

Observed or researched behaviours are abstracted into pictographic forms. Translation here does not mean converting an English word into an image. It means moving from behaviour to relationship, and from relationship to a repeatable sign.

### 5. Build a Coherent Visual System

Individual symbols are organised into a consistent visual language with shared formal principles.

### 6. Test the System Through Interaction

The communication proposition is extended into learning and digital interaction. The website allows the audience to encounter the symbols as something to interpret and use rather than simply view.

### 7. Situate the System in a Narrative World

World-building demonstrates why the system would be needed and what kind of relationship its use might make possible.

### Methodological Reading

The project can also be understood through Kylie's broader working pattern:

```text
Discover → Connect → Translate → Materialise
```

Within TYRANNO:

- **Discover:** investigate species, communication, semiotics, and context;
- **Connect:** identify relationships between behaviour, meaning, symbol, and user;
- **Translate:** convert those relationships into a pictographic communication logic;
- **Materialise:** build the visual system, motion, interaction, website, and speculative experience.

This framework describes the real relationships between phases of work. It should not be used to flatten the project into a rigid linear process; research, interpretation, and visual development informed one another throughout.

## 14. Intentional Design Decisions

The boundaries of TYRANNO are as important as its visible outputs. Several possible directions were deliberately excluded because they would weaken the project's core reasoning.

### Decision 01. Not a Complete Language

TYRANNO does not attempt to construct a complete grammar, vocabulary, and linguistic culture.

The reason is not simply limited production capacity. A complete language would introduce a substantial learning burden before communication could begin. Participants would need to invest significant time and effort in mastering a speculative system whose immediate purpose is to create an initial possibility of contact.

The project therefore prioritises an accessible entry point over linguistic completeness.

**Research implication:** communication requires a usable shared reference.  
**Decision:** develop a focused pictographic system rather than a total language.  
**Consequence:** the project remains an invitation to communication, not an obligation to master an invented language.

### Decision 02. Not a Conventional Typeface

Calling the outcome a typeface would imply that the project operates inside an established writing system.

**Conceptual implication:** the communication problem precedes writing.  
**Decision:** define the outcome as a pictographic symbol system.  
**Consequence:** visual forms can remain connected to T. rex behaviour and context rather than functioning as stylised substitutions for human letters.

### Decision 03. Not Human Language Translated

An English-to-TYRANNO translation model would assume that human language supplies the original and complete meaning.

**Ethical implication:** this would make human expression the default measure of the other species.  
**Decision:** begin with shared reference rather than human vocabulary.  
**Consequence:** the system is organised around the species and the relationship, not around the authority of the human speaker.

### Decision 04. No Sound-Based Layer

A vocal communication layer was considered and rejected.

Research challenged the cinematic assumption that T. rex communication could be based on familiar roaring. The likely nature of its vocalisation, combined with the physical scale difference between humans and the animal, made a shout-based exchange an unreliable foundation for the system.

**Research implication:** popular representations do not provide a sufficient communication model.  
**Decision:** do not build the primary system around sound.  
**Consequence:** the project concentrates on visible, learnable, behaviour-based reference.

### Decision 05. Not Automatic Translation

TYRANNO does not remove the participant from the act of interpretation.

Automatic translation could make the interaction appear frictionless, but it would conceal the project's central question: how does someone enter a communicative relationship with an unfamiliar other?

**Conceptual implication:** communication is not only an output; it is a negotiated process.  
**Decision:** include learning and active interpretation.  
**Consequence:** the user must choose to engage with the system rather than receive an invisible technical solution.

### Decision 06. Not AI-Dependent

AI is not presented as the origin or essential mechanism of TYRANNO.

The project's theory of shared reference was established through its original communication investigation. A later experience of working with AI tools provided a useful parallel: even detailed instructions may produce divergent interpretations when sender and receiver do not share the same reference.

**Historical implication:** the AI experience validates an existing idea; it does not retroactively originate the project.  
**Decision:** retain AI as a reflection and comparison, not as an unsupported core technology.  
**Consequence:** the project's historical reasoning remains accurate.

### Decision 07. No Claim of Scientific Resolution

The project does not claim that T. rex cognition or cross-species language has been scientifically reconstructed.

**Epistemic implication:** speculative design creates propositions, not proof.  
**Decision:** make the fictional and exploratory status legible.  
**Consequence:** TYRANNO can ask a serious communication question without overstating the certainty of its answer.

## 16. What Learning TYRANNO Means

Learning TYRANNO is not presented as a compulsory adaptation imposed on every human within the speculative world.

It is a choice.

To learn the system is to choose to:

- enter a shared environment consciously;
- recognise another species as a participant rather than only an object;
- invest effort in understanding unfamiliar behaviour;
- allow another form of life to affect one's own actions;
- attempt communication where avoidance or control would be easier;
- accept coexistence as a relationship that requires negotiation.

The learning process therefore carries conceptual meaning beyond usability.

It transforms the participant from an observer of the project into someone who must decide whether communication is worth the effort. The system does not guarantee mutual understanding. It offers a structure through which the attempt can begin.

This distinction is central:

> TYRANNO does not force communication. It makes communication available as a choice.

The act of learning becomes evidence of openness. It signals a willingness to leave the security of a familiar human system and enter a context in which meaning must be negotiated with another species.

## 17. Final Outcome

The final outcome of TYRANNO is not a solved language between humans and dinosaurs.

It is a coherent speculative proposition:

> Cross-species communication may begin when participants can orient interpretation around a shared reference, even before they possess a shared language.

The project demonstrates this proposition through a pictographic system, a visual identity, behavioural animation, interactive learning, a website, and narrative world-building.

Its success should not be judged by whether it proves exactly how a real T. rex would communicate. It should be judged by whether the system makes its conceptual argument legible:

- familiar language cannot be assumed;
- visual form must respond to the other species;
- meaning depends on relationships between signs, referents, users, and contexts;
- participation requires learning;
- communication creates a possibility for coexistence without erasing difference.

TYRANNO ultimately materialises a question rather than closing it. It gives form to the space between two participants who do not yet understand one another.

## 18. Designer Reflection

TYRANNO changed the designer's understanding of where communication design begins.

The project began with the apparent task of producing a visual language. It gradually revealed that visual language was already too late in the process. Before a designer can decide how a message should look, the designer must understand:

- who is communicating;
- what context connects them;
- which assumptions they do not share;
- what can function as a common reference;
- what relationship the communication is intended to make possible.

This shifted the project from form-making to contextual construction.

It also exposed a stable pattern in Kylie's practice: she does not treat context as background information added before design begins. Context changes the definition of the design problem itself. When the context changes, categories such as language, typography, interaction, user, and outcome must be reconsidered.

### The AI Parallel

A later experience of communicating with AI tools gave the project's original theory a contemporary and personal confirmation.

The sender may believe an instruction is highly specific. The receiving system may process every word and still produce an unexpected result. Repeating the message with more detail does not necessarily resolve the difference, because the problem may not be a lack of information. The two sides may be organising meaning through different internal references.

This experience clarified the practical force of TYRANNO's central proposition:

> Precision of expression cannot compensate fully for the absence of shared reference.

The parallel does not turn TYRANNO into an AI project. It demonstrates that the project's communication problem is not confined to its speculative dinosaur scenario. The same instability can appear whenever participants assume that transmission guarantees understanding.

### What Changed in the Designer's Practice

After TYRANNO, communication can no longer be treated as the final presentation layer of a project.

Communication becomes a structure that must be designed from context outward:

```text
Context
  ↓
Participants
  ↓
Relationship
  ↓
Shared Reference
  ↓
Communication
  ↓
Visual Form
```

This does not mean that every future project must use the same diagram or reach the same conclusion. It means that visual form must be accountable to the relationships and conditions that produce it.

The project therefore contributed more than an artefact to Kylie's practice. It established a way of asking questions:

- What context has been assumed?
- Whose system is being treated as the default?
- What relationship needs to be made possible?
- What can the participants genuinely share?
- Which medium grows from those conditions?

These questions continue beyond TYRANNO.

## 21. Defendable Claims

The following claims may be used in a portfolio review, interview, presentation, or public case study when phrased with the limits preserved here.

### Claim 01

**Communication requires more than the delivery of information.**

**Basis:** sender and receiver may interpret the same message through different standards.  
**Design response:** TYRANNO creates a shared frame of reference grounded in observable behaviour.  
**Project evidence:** the symbol system derives meaning from T. rex rather than from an imported human alphabet.

### Claim 02

**A shared language is not the only possible starting point for communication.**

**Basis:** TYRANNO's participants cannot be assumed to possess equivalent languages.  
**Design response:** the system begins with observable bodies, behaviours, and relationships.  
**Project evidence:** pictographic symbols precede any claim to complete grammar or vocabulary.

### Claim 03

**The context of communication should determine its form.**

**Basis:** species difference, physical scale, environment, and coexistence change what communication requires.  
**Design response:** the project rejects a generic human-centred language solution.  
**Project evidence:** symbols, interaction, and narrative are organised around the speculative cross-species context.

### Claim 04

**Pictographic symbols are more appropriate than a conventional typeface for this project.**

**Basis:** a typeface assumes an existing writing system.  
**Design response:** visual forms retain a relationship with bodies, actions, and behaviour.  
**Project evidence:** T. rex functions as the reference scale of the symbol system.

### Claim 05

**Learning is part of the project's meaning, not only its interface.**

**Basis:** participation requires a voluntary effort to understand an unfamiliar other.  
**Design response:** the project includes learning and translation interactions rather than invisible automatic mediation.  
**Project evidence:** the user enters the communication system through active interpretation.

### Claim 06

**The absence of a complete language and sound layer is intentional.**

**Basis:** a complete language would create excessive entry cost; vocal communication lacked a sufficiently credible foundation for the project's primary system.  
**Design response:** TYRANNO concentrates on an accessible, behaviour-based pictographic system.  
**Project evidence:** exclusions reinforce the project's purpose rather than representing missing production.

### Claim 07

**TYRANNO is speculative, not scientifically conclusive.**

**Basis:** the project constructs a proposition about communication rather than claiming verified dinosaur cognition.  
**Design response:** research informs the system while fiction remains legible.  
**Project evidence:** world-building, interaction, and symbols operate as an exploratory communication scenario.

### Medium Summary

TYRANNO investigates cross-species communication through a speculative future in which humans and T. rex occupy a shared environment. Rather than translating human language into a fictional dinosaur alphabet, the project uses the animal's body, posture, movement, and behaviour as the reference scale for a pictographic symbol system. Research, semiotics, animation, interaction, and digital storytelling come together to examine how shared reference can make communication—and a negotiated relationship—possible before shared language exists.

### Case-Study Introduction

How can communication begin when its participants share neither language nor scale?

TYRANNO responds to this question through a speculative communication system for humans and T. rex. The project treats the dinosaur not as a visual theme, but as the source of the system's meaning. Its body and behaviour become shared points of reference from which pictographic symbols, motion, learning interactions, and a digital experience are developed.

The outcome is not a claim to have solved cross-species language. It is a designed proposition: communication may become possible when difference is not erased, but organised around something both participants can encounter.

### Terms Requiring Qualification

Use *typeface* only when discussing the project's early framing or typography-related visual development. Do not use it as the complete definition of the final system.

Use *language* carefully. TYRANNO proposes a focused communication system; it does not claim to have produced a complete language.

Use *translation* to describe a designed interaction or the movement between behaviour, meaning, and symbol. Do not imply scientifically verified translation of dinosaur cognition.

Use *AI* only in the designer reflection or where supported by a documented later experiment. Do not describe AI as the historical origin or required mechanism of TYRANNO.

Use *AR* as a proposed extension unless a completed implementation is verified by project evidence.

### Claims to Avoid

Do not claim that:

- the system enables scientifically proven communication with T. rex;
- the symbols reproduce a verified dinosaur language;
- T. rex behaviour or vocalisation is known with complete certainty;
- the project produced a complete grammar or vocabulary;
- AI generated or autonomously designed the communication system;
- the speculative scenario is a prediction;
- untested outcomes demonstrate measurable behavioural impact.

## 25. Closing Statement

TYRANNO is not ultimately about whether humans could teach a dinosaur to read.

It is about what happens when a familiar communication system can no longer be assumed.

By placing two radically different species in a shared context, the project makes the hidden foundations of communication visible. Meaning requires reference. Reference depends on context. Communication develops through relationships between bodies, signs, interpreters, and environments.

The pictographic system gives those relationships form. The learning experience asks the participant to enter them. The speculative world shows why that choice matters.

TYRANNO therefore stands as a project about the beginning of communication: the moment before language is shared, when one participant decides to recognise another and attempts to build a frame in which understanding might become possible.
