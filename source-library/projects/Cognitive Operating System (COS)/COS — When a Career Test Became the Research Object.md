# COS — When a Career Test Became the Research Object

**Project type:** Self-initiated human–AI research and assessment-design experiment  
**Role:** Participant, test co-designer and critical reviewer  
**Scope:** First testing and revision cycle, 1 August 2026

## Starting question

Could a conversational test reveal how someone approaches problems and decisions without relying first on their education, job history or a personality label?

The AI initially proposed questions and interpretations. I responded to the scenarios, but repeatedly found that some questions could not support the conclusions they were intended to produce. The test itself became the object of investigation.

## What failed

Different questions failed for different reasons:

- **Undefined world:** In a scenario set at a car brand, the answer depended on whether the work happened inside the brand or at an agency. Without that distinction, the same response could mean different things.
- **Missing role and authority:** A scenario treated my response as a stable personal tendency, although I was answering as the person responsible for the project. A different role would change the relevant responsibilities and likely change my response.
- **Ambiguous feedback:** A senior stakeholder’s brief reaction could not be interpreted reliably without first identifying what work had been shown and what the feedback referred to.

These were not simply “difficult questions.” They exposed cases where the test risked measuring its own assumptions, wording or interpretation rather than the intended aspect of decision-making.

## How the method changed

Each failure was recorded as a design requirement. The developing protocol called for a defined **world, role, authority and constraints**; clearer separation of the variable being tested; and conditional branches where several responses could be valid. Later prompts also asked for a *brain log*—an account of the respondent’s immediate sequence of attention and questions—rather than only a polished final answer.

The working cycle became: **propose a question → observe the response → identify what the question actually measured → revise or reject it → retain the reason.**

## Result and boundary

The first cycle produced a more explicit set of question-design and interpretation rules. Its value was the documented failure-and-revision process, not a validated cognitive profile.

In the conversation, the AI retrospectively estimated that only around **4–6 of 23** first-day questions met its intended measurement goal, later expressing this as **5/23**. That is an internal, subjective review—not an independently verified success rate. The experiment involved one participant and does not establish validity across other people.
