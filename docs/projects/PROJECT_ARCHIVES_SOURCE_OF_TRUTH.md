# 1. TYRANNO

## Canonical identity

**Public title:** TYRANNO  
**Working subtitle:** A speculative communication system for human and T. rex coexistence in 2050  
**Domain:** Speculative Communication Design; Semiotics; Typography and Visual Language; Interaction Design  
**Project context:** Master's thesis / final design project at NABA, Milan  
**Authorship framing:** Individual design-research project developed within an academic context

## Core project definition

TYRANNO imagines a 2050 urban context in which humans and Tyrannosaurus rex must coexist after de-extinction or biological reconstruction. The project begins from a communication problem: human language assumes human anatomy, perception and speech. It therefore cannot simply be transferred to a radically different species.

The project develops a behaviour-driven symbolic language and typeface system. It translates posture, movement, gesture and inferred behavioural signals into a communication structure that humans can learn and interpret without treating spoken language as the default channel.

## Historical starting point

The project did not begin from the formal ambition to design a typeface. It began from a speculative future context and the question of what communication would require inside that context. Typography became one component of a larger communication system.

## Core question

**How might humans communicate with a species that does not share human language, anatomy or perceptual assumptions?**

## Research foundations

Confirmed research areas:

- semiotics, including icon, index and symbol relationships;
- visual, pictographic, ideographic and logographic language systems;
- gesture and non-verbal communication;
- T. rex anatomy, posture, gait, head and tail movement and behavioural hypotheses;
- speculative design;
- practice-based design research;
- typography as a behavioural and communicative system rather than only letterform styling.

Confirmed theoretical references in the thesis include Saussure, Peirce, Eco, Dunne & Raby, Malpass and related sources.

## Concept development

The central concept translates observed or inferred T. rex behaviours into a human-readable symbolic system.

Confirmed principles:

- the system is behaviour-driven rather than phonetic;
- motion, posture and gesture function as communication material;
- icons, indexes and symbols operate together;
- the system develops from a smaller core set into an extended language;
- static glyphs and animated demonstrations are connected;
- the project is a communication system, not only a type specimen.

## Process

Confirmed process components:

- behavioural and anatomical research;
- extraction of repeatable visual features;
- symbol sketching and classification;
- iterative glyph development;
- testing relationships between static form, movement and meaning;
- development of alphabetic, symbolic and extended sets;
- interactive website design;
- translation, animation and gamified learning explorations;
- later AR application proposals.

## Final outcome

Confirmed outputs:

- symbolic language / typeface system;
- core and extended glyph sets;
- animated or motion-based communication demonstrations;
- interactive website with translation and learning interactions;
- thesis book;
- final academic presentation;
- proposed AR extensions.

## Role and contribution

Safe canonical role line:

> Concept Development, Design Research, Visual-System Design, Typeface and Glyph Development, Interaction Design and Project Production

The work may be described as an individual project developed with academic supervision. It should not be framed as a commercial commission.

## Current interpretation

TYRANNO demonstrates the ability to:

- begin from a speculative context rather than a predetermined visual output;
- question human-centred communication assumptions;
- turn research into a coherent visual and interactive system;
- connect concept, narrative, typography, motion and interaction;
- use design to create the conditions in which a new form of communication becomes meaningful.

# 2. THE INFINITE DISCUSSION

## Canonical identity

**Public title:** The Infinite Discussion  
**Original working title:** Debate  
**Domain:** Machine Vision; AI Behaviour; Interactive / Installation Design; Visual Communication  
**Project context:** Academic group project in which Kylie originated the concept, led the project and was effectively the only active contributor

## Core project definition

The Infinite Discussion is an installation exploring what happens when two conversational AI systems are assigned opposing identities and allowed to continue a discussion with minimal human intervention.

The project does not primarily ask how machines literally see. It reframes machine vision as machine perspective: how a machine appears to think, maintain a position and respond to another system with a conflicting position.

## Historical development

### Phase 1 — Dashcam concept

The original brief was `Machine Vision`.

The first interpretation was literal:

Machine Vision → Machine as Camera → What can cameras capture?

Inspired by a Milan public-awareness campaign showing the final smiles of suicide victims, the initial proposal examined genuine facial expressions at the moment of traffic accidents through interior and exterior dashcam footage.

The proposal was rejected because:

- the footage would be difficult or impossible to obtain;
- it raised serious privacy concerns;
- the imagery risked becoming ethically disturbing;
- most importantly, the concept remained a shallow and literal interpretation of machine vision.

This rejection became the turning point.

### Phase 2 — Vision as perspective

Kylie reframed `vision` from eyesight to perspective.

The project shifted from:

> How do machines see?

To:

> How do machines hold and express a point of view?

This shift occurred during an earlier stage of conversational AI, when systems were less flexible, more likely to insist on their initial position and less willing to revise an opinion.

### Phase 3 — Two AI systems

Kylie became interested in what might happen if two AI systems, each inclined to maintain its own view, interacted with one another.

The original observational aim was to discover whether they would eventually recognise that the other participant was also a machine.

Completely unconstrained dialogue did not continue reliably. The systems were therefore assigned opposing identities and positions. Male and female voices were selected to give the conversation human-like social cues while preserving the fact that both speakers were machines.

The project evolved from a `debate` toward an `infinite discussion`: a loop in which opposing positions continue without a predetermined resolution.

## Installation evolution

The installation changed substantially while preserving the same conceptual intention.

Early vision:

- a conference room;
- ordinary furniture and objects behaving as speaking machines;
- an atmosphere inspired by animated talking furniture;
- objects conversing naturally without a visible human operator.

Later development:

- simplified from many animated objects to machines;
- reduced to two computers;
- placed inside a large transparent acrylic enclosure;
- presented as a sealed, museum-like environment;
- visitors remained outside and listened through headphones.

The enclosure communicated that the discussion occurred without outside intervention.

## Production compromise

The intended version used live networked AI conversation.

Because live networking and exhibition reliability were insufficient, the final installation used:

- a pre-generated conversation approximately three hours long;
- two synchronised looping videos;
- alternating male and female voices;
- captions;
- simulated listening, recognising and speaking interface states;
- precise timing to create the experience of two machines conversing in real time.

This was a technical compromise, not a conceptual change.

## Core question

**What emerges when two machines are given conflicting human-like identities and allowed to maintain their own perspectives?**

## Role and authorship

Canonical role line:

> Concept Originator, Team Lead, Interaction and Installation Concept, Conversation-System Design, Visual Interface Design and Final Production

Authorship must reflect that the project was officially collaborative but that Kylie originated the concept, led the team and carried the effective development responsibility. Any named collaborators must still be credited once confirmed.

## Final outcome

Confirmed final format:

- two-computer installation;
- transparent acrylic enclosure;
- headphone-based audience listening experience;
- synchronised AI conversation simulation;
- approximately three-hour looped discussion;
- male and female synthetic voices;
- captions and conversational-state interface animations.

## Current interpretation

The project is not best framed as an editorial system for organising multiple human viewpoints.

Its stronger and historically accurate interpretation is:

- machine vision reframed as machine perspective;
- AI behaviour observed through a designed conversational context;
- human-like identity assigned to machines to expose similarities and differences between machine and human interaction;
- a project that creates the conditions for behaviour to emerge rather than scripting a predetermined conclusion.

A useful historical progression is:

Machine Vision → Vision → Perspective → Conflicting Perspectives → Resistance to Revision → Infinite Discussion

## Methodological observation

Within this project, Kylie repeatedly prioritised:

- minimising intervention;
- creating a closed context;
- allowing interaction to unfold;
- observing what state the system became;
- preserving the possibility of an unplanned result.

This is evidence of an emergence-oriented approach inside this project. It may connect to other projects, but it must not yet be presented as a universal personal methodology.

## Publication boundaries

- Do not describe the work as a live AI installation unless the final exhibited version was in fact live.
- Clearly distinguish the intended live system from the final synchronised simulation.
- Do not claim the AI systems independently chose their identities; opposing identities were assigned to sustain the conversation.
- Do not reduce the project to a neutral multi-perspective editorial exercise.
- Preserve the rejected dashcam concept because it explains the genuine conceptual shift.

- exact installation dimensions;
- precise software and AI systems used;
- final exhibition or presentation context;
- public-safe installation photographs, interface files and conversation excerpts;

# Cross-project observations

The following observations are supported by the current archive but are not yet a final declared methodology.

## Context before output

Across the documented histories, Kylie often begins by reconstructing or defining the context in which communication must operate before deciding what form the output should take.

Examples:

- TYRANNO defines a future coexistence context before designing a language;
- The Infinite Discussion reframes the meaning of `vision` before designing an installation;
- Deear must be understood through its editorial context before its layouts can be interpreted accurately;
- Madam uses storyboard and sequence context before the individual images are produced;
- the Audi archive requires employer, client, audience and production context before authorship can be described responsibly.

## Conditions, context and emergence

A recurring project-level pattern is:

1. understand or construct a context;
2. design conditions inside that context;
3. allow behaviour, communication or interpretation to emerge;
4. observe and refine rather than forcing a predetermined conclusion.

This is strongly evidenced in The Infinite Discussion and meaningfully present in TYRANNO. It remains an observation across the wider archive rather than a final personal doctrine.

## Historical context before interpretation

Future writing must preserve the historical sequence through which each project developed. Later interpretations may clarify the work, but they must not erase rejected directions, technical compromises or authorship boundaries that explain why the final outcome exists.
