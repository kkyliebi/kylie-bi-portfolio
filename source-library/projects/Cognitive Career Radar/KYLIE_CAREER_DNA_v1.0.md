# 01｜Core Identity

## Professional identity

Kylie Bi is a multidisciplinary communication creative working across conceptual thinking, visual communication, narrative systems, creative production, brand communication, speculative design, and interdisciplinary storytelling.

Her strongest professional position is not a single conventional title. It is a **Creative Communication Hybrid**: someone who can move between research, strategy, concept development, communication design, production, and execution.

A useful market-facing formulation is:

> **Communication Designer & Creative Producer**

A broader formulation is:

> **Communication Creative working across concept development, visual communication, narrative systems, and creative production.**

The deeper professional identity is:

> **A translator of possibilities.**

This is not a job title. It describes a recurring way of working: discovering possibilities or relationships that are not immediately visible, understanding their logic, and translating them into forms that people can understand, experience, discuss, or implement.

---

# 02｜Career Engine

Kylie's career should be understood through a process rather than through a title:

> **Ambiguous Problem → Understand → Deconstruct → Structure → Conceptualise → Translate → Coordinate → Produce → Realise**

In shorter form:

> **Understand → Structure → Concept → Translation → Realisation**

Kylie's value does not begin with a predefined visual medium. She is strongest when she can enter a complex or ambiguous problem, understand the underlying structure, determine what actually needs to be solved, develop a meaningful concept, translate that concept into a communication system, and help move it toward implementation.

She is not primarily an executor of predetermined answers. She is most valuable when she can participate in understanding the problem, defining or reframing it, developing the conceptual response, structuring communication, connecting disciplines, translating ideas between teams, coordinating creative production, solving implementation constraints, and protecting coherence through execution.

# 37 | Reference Ecosystem: Creative-led Organisations
Kylie’s preferred professional ecosystem is not defined primarily by industry, company size, or conventional job titles. It is better understood through the structural role of creativity within an organisation.
 
The organisations that repeatedly demonstrate stronger potential alignment are those in which creative problem solving is structurally central to the organisation’s value creation.

**Reference cases include:**
**Alkemy**+ — Creative + Strategy + Technology + Business Transformation
**Atoms** — Technology + Physical World + Systems + First-Principles Problem Solving
**WOA** — Creative Direction + Technology + Production + Physical Experience
**Race Service** — Automotive + Culture + Creative + Talent + Media + Community
**Circular** — Creative Direction + Strategy + Branding + Experience + Production + Management
**BorromeoDeSilva** — Art Direction + Brand + Visual Culture + Creative Direction
**Landor** — Brand Strategy + Design + Experience + Transformation
**AKQA **— Creativity + Technology + Strategy + Experience
**Tutto Bene** — Creative Studio + Brand/Culture + Art Direction + Visual Communication

**Across these organisations, several recurring structural characteristics become visible:**

**1. Creative is a core business function.**
Creativity is not merely a supporting layer attached to another business model.

**2. Ideas function as products.**
Concepts, strategies, narratives, identities, experiences, systems, and creative solutions are themselves part of the value delivered.

**3. Disciplines interact.**
Creative work frequently operates across Strategy, Design, Technology, Production, Brand, Management, and Communication.

**4. Problems precede media.**
The organisation begins with a problem, opportunity, or ambition and determines the appropriate medium or solution afterwards.

**5. Realisation matters.**
Conceptual thinking is expected to move beyond abstraction into something executable, communicable, experiential, or operational.

**6. Professional identities can remain hybrid.**
These environments make space for people whose contribution crosses conventional categories such as Designer, Strategist, Producer, Project Manager, or Creative.

This ecosystem therefore provides a more useful reference model for Kylie than a conventional industry hierarchy.

**PROFESSIONAL ECOSYSTEM**
├── BRAND-SIDE ORGANISATIONS
│     └── Possible fit; depends on role and decision ownership
│
├── CREATIVE-LED ORGANISATIONS
│     ├── Studio
│     ├── Agency
│     └── Consultancy
│           └── Multidisciplinary problem solving
│
└── LARGE ADVERTISING NETWORK
└── Conditional fit; depends heavily on team, department, role, and working model

**Reference principle**
Creative-led ecosystem = stronger prior probability of fit.

This does not mean that “creative” automatically means good, or that corporate organisations automatically mean bad. The relevant question is whether creativity, problem solving, interdisciplinary collaboration, and realisation are structurally central to the organisation’s work.

**Agent-level rule**
When discovering organisations for Kylie, prioritise organisations whose core business is built around creative problem solving, strategy, design, communication, experience, innovation, or interdisciplinary production.

Do not use brand-side status, company size, prestige, or the presence of a “creative” department as proxies for fit.

**Evaluate instead:**
What problems does the organisation solve → how does it solve them → what role does creativity play → how are disciplines organised → where does decision ownership sit → how closely does that structure match Kylie’s Career Engine?

**Career DNA is not a fixed label. It is an operating model that becomes more accurate through real-world testing.**
